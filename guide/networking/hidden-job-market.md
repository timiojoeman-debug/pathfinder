# The Hidden Job Market

> **Prerequisites:** none — start here.
> **Concept id:** `c-hidden-job-market` · **Sources:** Hidden Job Market Tactics & Tips; Wiki/concepts/hidden-job-market.md

## What this is

The hidden job market is the **70–80% of roles that are filled before any public posting ever appears** — through internal candidates, hiring-manager networks, employee referrals, and recruiter outreach. The public job board is where the *leftover* 20–30% lands, and where 1,000+ applicants fight over each one.

## Why it matters

If most roles never reach a job board, then the job board is the **most crowded, lowest-yield channel you can pick**. Cold applying isn't "doing everything right and being unlucky" — it's competing in the employer's last-resort channel. Understanding this reframes your whole strategy: stop optimising your position in the 1,000-applicant pile, and start getting in *before* the pile exists.

## How it works

Companies prefer the hidden market because it is faster, cheaper, and lower-risk:

- Trusted contacts reduce screening effort.
- Referred hires are higher quality and stay longer.
- Referrals and internal hires cut time-to-fill from **3–4 months to ~2 weeks**.

You tap it proactively:

1. **Build a target list** of 20–30 employers.
2. **Follow them** on LinkedIn and mark "interested" — this triggers alerts and signals to recruiters.
3. **Connect with current employees** in your target role *before* a vacancy exists.
4. **Get referred** once they know you.

For first-years specifically, **small startups (2–50 staff)** are the most accessible entry point.

## Common mistakes

- Only applying to roles **after** they're publicly posted.
- Treating volume as the strategy — more cold applications in the worst channel.
- Waiting for a vacancy before talking to anyone at the company.

## Exercises

1. List your top **20–30 target employers** (mix of big tech, startups, and fintechs you'd genuinely join).
2. Follow all of them on LinkedIn and mark "interested" on any open roles.
3. For your top 5, find **one current employee** in your target role (alumni first).

## Reflection questions

- What share of your effort this month went into the public-board channel vs. the hidden market?
- Which target company could you become "known at" before its next role opens?

## Real examples

- **Good:** Following a fintech for two months, commenting on a manager's post, then being warm when a junior role drops.
- **Bad:** Discovering a role the day it closes and firing off an untailored application into the 1,000-applicant pile.
