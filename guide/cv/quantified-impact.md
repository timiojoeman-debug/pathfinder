# Quantified Impact

> **Prerequisites:** [Proof of Work](proof-of-work.md)
> **Concept id:** `c-quantified-impact` · **Sources:** TechTalk 2026 CV Blueprint; Wiki/concepts/proof-of-work.md

## What this is

Quantified Impact is writing experience as **achievements, not responsibilities**: every bullet is **action verb + task + a quantified result**, with at least **two metrics per role**. It's proof of work expressed at the bullet level.

## Why it matters

The Experience section is where recruiters spend the most of their 6–8 seconds. A duty ("responsible for the CI pipeline") tells them nothing they can weigh; an achievement ("cut build time 40%") gives them a number to react to. Numbers are what survive the scan — they convert "this person did stuff" into "this person produced results."

## How it works

For each bullet, ask three questions:

1. **What did I do?** → a strong action verb (built, cut, shipped, automated).
2. **On what?** → the task or system.
3. **With what result?** → a number: %, time saved, users served, revenue, latency.

If a bullet has no number, either find one (estimate honestly) or cut it. Aim for **≥2 quantified bullets per role**. For students, quantify projects and academics: "served 200 users," "92% average," "reduced API latency 30%."

## Common mistakes

- Bullets that list tasks with no outcome ("worked on the frontend").
- Adjective-led claims ("highly skilled in…") with no measurable result.
- One token metric per role and duties for the rest.

## Exercises

1. Audit your CV: count how many bullets contain a number. Aim to double it.
2. Rewrite your three weakest bullets into verb + task + result.
3. For your strongest project, find **two** honest numbers to attach.

## Reflection questions

- Which of your bullets would a recruiter remember three seconds after reading?
- Where are you describing effort when you could be describing outcome?

## Real examples

- **Good:** "Cut CI build time 40% by parallelising test stages, unblocking ~15 daily deploys."
- **Bad:** "Responsible for maintaining the team's CI pipeline."
