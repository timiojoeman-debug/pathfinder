# Proof of Work

> **Prerequisites:** none — start here.
> **Concept id:** `c-proof-of-work` · **Sources:** TechTalk Day 1/2 (CV Blueprint); Wiki/concepts/proof-of-work.md

## What this is

Proof of Work is the 2026 hiring bar: not "qualified," but **fit + demonstrable evidence**. Every claim is replaced with a receipt — *show, don't tell*. "No proof? Filtered out."

## Why it matters

Recruiters read only the top **20–30%** of a CV and want to justify interviewing you *fast*. Evidence makes that justification instant; an unbacked claim is indistinguishable from everyone else's. In a market where AI lets anyone *claim* anything, the only thing that separates you is what a recruiter can **see, click, and verify**.

## How it works

Apply "show, don't tell" at every layer a recruiter sees:

- **CV** — metrics, not duties (action verb + task + result).
- **LinkedIn** — measurable impact + featured work.
- **Portfolio** — receipts: live demo links and your real numbers.
- **Interview** — thought process + quantified STARL results.
- **AI fluency** — a workflow you built and can describe.

For students with thin experience: a GitHub link in the header, a metric on education ("92% average"), and "Live Demo" links on projects. The single highest-value proof is **shipping a real project with real users**.

## Common mistakes

- "Responsible for X" instead of "achieved Y by doing X."
- A skills list of claimed technologies with nothing clickable.
- Treating the CV as a duties record rather than an evidence file.

## Exercises

1. Take three CV bullets and rewrite each as **action verb + task + quantified result**.
2. Add **one clickable proof** above the fold (GitHub or a live demo).
3. Put a **real number** on every project and on your education line.

## Reflection questions

- For each claim on your CV, what could a recruiter click to verify it?
- What's the one piece of proof you could *build* this month that would outweigh ten claims?

## Real examples

- **Good:** GitHub in the header, "92% average," a Projects section with live demos and real user counts.
- **Bad:** "Highly skilled in React and Node" with no link, repo, or number anywhere.
