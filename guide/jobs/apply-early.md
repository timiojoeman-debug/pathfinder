# Apply Early

> **Prerequisites:** none — start here.
> **Concept id:** `c-apply-early` · **Sources:** Operation FAANG Report; Wiki/concepts/apply-early.md; 10 Interviews in 30 Days

## What this is

Apply Early is the idea that **timing is a strategy in itself**. Because nearly every employer uses **rolling admissions**, the first day a posting is live is the best day to apply — applying in the first **48–72 hours** can **3–5× your callback rate**.

## Why it matters

Recruiters work the inbox top-down and assessment quotas fill up. An application sent on day one is read against an emptier pile by a fresher reader; one sent on day ten competes against hundreds already in the queue. Some roles barely have a day ten — parts of Jane Street, certain Meta New Grad postings, and Google's mid-October window can fill in days. Timing is the most controllable lever you have, and most candidates waste it.

## How it works

The catch: applying early requires being **ready before the posting opens**. That means CV variants, pre-filled autofill, lined-up references, and a tracker — all prepared in advance — so when a target drops you tailor your master CV and submit within a couple of hours.

The deeper play: for the fastest-filling roles, the real edge is **already being warm** — in the recruiter's CRM from a prior cycle or event. That's where this domain meets [networking](../networking/README.md).

## Common mistakes

- Polishing one application for a week while the OA quota fills.
- Discovering fast-filling roles only after they've closed.
- "I'll do it properly at the weekend" — and the window shuts on Friday.

## Exercises

1. Pick three target employers and note their **typical opening month**.
2. Assemble an **application-day kit**: master CV, one tailored variant, autofill set up, references lined up.
3. Set up the **monitoring stack** so you learn about openings on day one (see the [domain README](README.md)).

## Reflection questions

- For your top target, are you ready to apply within two hours of it opening — today?
- Where are you trading a *perfect* late application for a *good* early one (the wrong trade)?

## Real examples

- **Good:** Kit ready, you apply to Google within 48 hours of its window opening.
- **Bad:** You wait to "do it properly," and the 2–4 week window closes before you submit.
